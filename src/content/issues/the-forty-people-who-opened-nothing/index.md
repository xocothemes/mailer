Pull up your list and filter for addresses with no opens in twelve months. For most newsletters past their second year, it is somewhere between a quarter and a half.

The standard advice is a win-back sequence: four emails with increasingly plaintive subject lines, ending in "is this goodbye?". I have run those. They convert around two percent, and the two percent were mostly going to open the next issue anyway.

Here is the case for the other approach.

## What a dormant subscriber costs

**They cost you money.** Every provider prices on list size. A thousand dormant addresses on a typical plan is real money a year, spent on people who are not reading.

**They cost you deliverability.** This is the expensive one. Mailbox providers score you on how recipients behave. A third of your sends landing on addresses that never open is a third of your sends saying "this mail is unwanted". That score is applied to _everyone_, including the readers who love you.

**They cost you clarity.** An open rate calculated against a list that is a third dead is a number you cannot use. Every decision you make from it is made through a fog.

## What removing them actually did

I suppressed 1,340 addresses from a list of 4,200 in February. Nobody was deleted — suppressed, so the records exist and anyone who comes back is recognised.

- Open rate went from 28% to 44%. Same readers, honest denominator.
- Inbox placement in Gmail improved measurably; Promotions-tab share dropped by about a third.
- Provider bill dropped by 40 a month.
- Six people noticed and re-subscribed.

The list looked smaller on the dashboard and more people read the newsletter. Both of those were true at once, and only one of them mattered.

> A subscriber count is a vanity number the moment it stops tracking readers. The point at which that happens is earlier than anyone wants to believe.

## How to do it without being dramatic

**Define dormant honestly.** No opens _and_ no clicks in twelve months, for addresses that have received at least ten issues. A signup from three weeks ago is not dormant, it is new.

**Send one email. One.** Subject: "Still want this?" Body: three sentences and two links — keep it, or stop. No countdown, no guilt, no "we'll miss you". The four-email sequence exists to squeeze the two percent, and the squeezing is what makes people resent you.

**Suppress, do not delete.** Keep the record so you can tell a returning subscriber from a new one, and so you never mail them again by accident.

**Do it once a year.** On a date, in the calendar. It is hygiene, not a campaign.

## The part that is uncomfortable

You will watch a number you have spent years growing go down by a third in an afternoon. If you have ever quoted that number to a sponsor or on a pitch, it will feel like a loss.

It is not. It is the difference between the audience you have and the audience you report. Only one of those replies to you.
