Two issues ago I mentioned killing a design system I had paid a designer to build. Several of you asked for the numbers, so here they are.

## The test

Four months, alternating issues. Version A was the full template: header image, brand colours, section dividers, a footer with social icons and a card layout for links. Version B was one column of text, a single rule above the sign-off, links in the body, and nothing else.

Same writer, same lengths, same send times, same list. Not a clean experiment — alternating means seasonality is baked in — but eight issues each is enough to see a direction.

|                        | Template | Near-plain |
| ---------------------- | -------- | ---------- |
| Open rate              | 41.2%    | 43.8%      |
| Click rate             | 3.1%     | 4.9%       |
| Replies per issue      | 6        | 19         |
| Promotions tab (Gmail) | 68%      | 22%        |
| Unsubscribes per issue | 11       | 7          |

The open rate difference is inside the noise. The rest is not.

## Why the plain one wins

**It looks like mail.** Gmail's Promotions tab is not a spam filter; it is a classifier, and a header image with brand colours and a row of social icons is exactly the shape of a marketing email. Removing the furniture moved two thirds of my Gmail readers back into the primary inbox, which is worth more than every other change I have ever made.

**It renders everywhere.** There is no version of a one-column text email that breaks in Outlook 2016, in dark mode, at 320px, or with images disabled. The template broke in three of those four.

**It reads as a person.** The reply numbers are the real story. Nineteen versus six, from the same writing. People reply to letters. They do not reply to newsletters that look like product announcements.

## What "near-plain" actually means

Not literally plain text. It is still HTML, because you want links, emphasis and a reasonable line length. It is just restrained:

```html
<body style="margin: 0; padding: 24px 16px; background: #fff">
  <div style="max-width: 560px; margin: 0 auto; font: 16px/1.6 system-ui, sans-serif">
    <!-- the issue -->
  </div>
</body>
```

That is the whole template. A max width, a system font stack, a colour, and generous line height. Everything else is the writing.

Two rules I stuck to:

- No image above the first paragraph. The first thing in the message should be words.
- No tracking parameters on internal links. They make your URLs look like a campaign, and you can read the referrer anyway.

> If your email needs a design system, the thing you are sending is not a letter.

## The part I got wrong

I did keep one image per issue, in the middle, and I still think that is right. The argument against decoration is not an argument against photographs — it is an argument against chrome.
