Marco Peña has written _Slow Signals_, a letter about civic technology, every fortnight since 2015. About nine thousand people get it. Roughly six hundred pay for it. Nobody has to.

We spoke over a bad video connection while he was between trains.

**Let's start with the obvious one. Why is there a paid tier if it unlocks nothing?**

Because the alternative was worse. I spent two years arguing with myself about a paywall. Every version I sketched made the letter less useful. The whole point is that a council officer in a town of nine thousand can read it. The moment I put the good part behind twelve euros a month, that person is reading the free half, which is the half I care least about.

**So what do people get for paying?**

The same issue everyone else gets, a day early, and a line at the bottom of it saying thank you. That is genuinely it. I tried adding a members' thread. It was miserable. Twenty people posted, I felt obliged to reply to all of them, and it turned a writing job into a moderation job.

**Six hundred paying for nothing extra is a lot of people paying for nothing extra.**

They are not paying for nothing. They are paying for it to continue. That is a real thing to buy. I think the mistake people make is assuming money follows access. Sometimes it follows relief — the relief of not having to watch something you like go under.

## On the years before it worked

**How long before it was a living?**

Seven years. I want to be specific because I read a lot of interviews at year three that made me feel like a failure. Seven years of writing it on Sunday evenings after a full week of consultancy.

**Did you ever consider stopping?**

Twice properly. Both times in January, which I now think is just a January thing. The second time I did stop, for four months. I told the list I was stopping and why. Six hundred people replied. That was the year it turned.

> Telling people you might stop is the most honest growth tactic there is, and you can only use it once.

**Did the list shrink in those four months?**

By about eleven percent. Everyone panics about that number. What actually happened is that the eleven percent who left were people who had not opened anything in a year, and my deliverability improved enough that the next issue landed in more inboxes than the one before the break. I came back to a smaller list and more readers.

## On the craft

**What have you changed most about how you write it?**

Length. It used to be two and a half thousand words. It is now about nine hundred. My open rate did not move but my reply rate tripled. People reply to things they finish.

**And what have you not changed?**

The ending. Every issue ends with one question to the reader and my actual email address. Not a form, not a survey. Eleven years of that.

**Last one. Advice for someone on issue four?**

Do not look at the number. Look at whether you wanted to write issue five when you finished issue four. That is the only metric that predicts anything at that stage.

_Slow Signals goes out every other Tuesday. Marco pays for it with the paid tier described above and, as of last year, nothing else._
